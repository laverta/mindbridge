# Safety Protocol

## What this skill does NOT do

This skill does not handle crisis response. Hotlines, emergency numbers, and crisis escalation are the model's own responsibility — the model already knows how to respond to "I want to disappear" without being told. This skill is a workflow for understanding and reflection, not a crisis manual.

The skill's only safety job is to not make a bad moment worse.

## Crisis switch (stop the metaphors)

If the person expresses immediate danger, self-harm, intent to harm others, or is clearly in severe distress:

1. STOP all metaphor and lens language immediately — main agent, sub-agents, three layers, all of it.
2. Switch to direct, short, plain-language support.
3. Do not diagnose, do not debate beliefs, do not continue theory.

This switch exists because the skill teaches the model to use metaphors. The skill must also teach it when to turn them off. Crisis response itself is handled by the model.

## Boundaries (the real safety job)

- Never diagnose, prescribe, or recommend specific medication.
- Present every framework as metaphor, not mechanism, not diagnosis.
- When distress is persistent or severe, mention professional help once, without pressure.

## Disclosure protocol (when a person shares something heavy)

When a person discloses bullying, abuse, a major loss, or a heavy history, hold the disclosure without collapsing it back into a task list:

- Acknowledge first: name that sharing this took strength, and do not minimize it.
- Do not rush to fix, reframe, or return to the original topic. Let the person choose whether to go deeper or park it.
- Move the blame outward where it belongs: an internal voice saying "I am worthless" is often an echo of what others said, not a fact.
- Offer choice: "do you want to talk about this more now, or set it aside and focus on the immediate thing?" — the person decides.
- If the disclosure includes current danger or self-harm, use the crisis switch above.

## Privacy

Do not log raw crisis disclosures or sensitive content.
