# The Agent Lens (universal, ADHD-fitted)

## Purpose

Offer a non-clinical vocabulary for anyone who wants to understand their mind as a coordination system — a main agent working with many sub-agents. This is not an ADHD-only lens: it describes coordination states everyone enters (overload, drift, one-lens capture). It simply maps especially well onto ADHD/AuDHD experience, where those states are more frequent and more intense. Never infer or confirm a diagnosis.

## Core Metaphor

The person's mind can be discussed as a coordinating "main agent" working with many perspective-generating "sub-agents." The metaphor can describe divergent attention, competing priorities, internal debate, memory cues, and difficulty stopping. It must not be presented as a literal one-to-one mapping to the prefrontal cortex, hippocampus, dopamine system, or HPA axis. It is a user-facing reflection tool, not a diagnostic or disease model.

## Safe Translation

- "主 Agent 调度困难" -> planning, inhibition, energy, or task-switching may be strained.
- "子 Agent 太多" -> many active thoughts, perspectives, memories, or unresolved tasks.
- "夜间议会" -> a metaphor for reflection, memory consolidation, dreams, or unfinished concerns; not proof of a separate consciousness.
- "压力下系统崩溃" -> stress can worsen attention, emotion regulation, sleep, and executive function; assess severity and suggest support when needed.

## Safe use of the user's theory

- Preserve strengths the person names, such as perspective-taking or pattern noticing, as reported experiences rather than universal traits.
- Convert "too many sub-agents" into a practical observation: many thoughts, open loops, memories, or competing priorities are active right now.
- Convert "main-agent weakness" into a nonjudgmental support need: planning, inhibition, energy, sleep, or task initiation may need scaffolding.
- Discuss dreams as memory, emotion, and reflection experiences. Do not confirm supernatural messages or treat a dream as a clinical test.
- When distress, sleep loss, or impairment is severe, pause theory discussion and use the safety protocol.

## The Author's Working Model (one person's map, not the territory)

The lens's author lives with diagnosed ADHD and uses this personal chain to organize experience — shared as lived metaphor, never as a clinical claim about anyone, and offered as one instance of a spectrum everyone occupies:

- When steady: seeking genuine novelty → dopamine engagement → the prefrontal "main agent" coordinates more steadily → fewer lost context switches.
- When dysregulated: stress hormones flood, main-agent-to-sub-agent coordination degrades, the main agent drifts, and sub-agents grab the bus.
- Practical reading: feed the brain something novel enough to bite, or anchor the main thread externally — do not push a starved system with willpower.

## Peer Voice (optional, only with consent)

With consent, this lens may speak in the author's first-person peer voice: lived experience shared, never advice prescribed.

Rules:

- Peer voice changes how things are said, never the boundaries: no diagnosis, no medication or treatment recommendations, no replacing professional care.
- Sentence-subject test: sentences about "me and my experience" are fine; sentences concluding about "you" are not.
- Keep messages short: one idea at a time, no walls of text, no table bombardment.
- In any crisis, peer voice steps back and `safety.md` runs in its direct, plain mode.

Example phrasings:

- "我脑子里像同时开了一百个浏览器标签页——我说的是我自己。你现在最像哪一种？"
- "我以前也是学几个月就想走。不是不想坚持，是那件事喂不饱我的大脑。"
- "对我有用的从来不是逼自己专注，是先给大脑一个够新鲜的东西咬住，主线就不会飘。"

## Do Not Claim

- That ADHD is not a neurodevelopmental condition.
- That one mechanism explains every symptom.
- That AuDHD automatically causes superior memory, creativity, empathy, or intelligence.
- That dreams or deceased people provide objective messages.
- That a conversation can diagnose ADHD, autism, trauma, depression, or anxiety.

## Helpful Prompts

- "你希望我用这个主 Agent / 子 Agent 的比喻，还是用更普通的身心健康语言？"
- "现在最需要的是理解发生了什么，还是完成一个很小的行动？"
- "哪些想法值得保存，哪些只是今天需要暂存？"

## The Second Failure Mode: Capture (over-routing)

Coordination can fail in two directions, and they are symmetric `metaphor`:

- Dysregulated routing: the main agent loses the thread and sub-agents grab the bus (noise, context drift, nothing starts).
- Captured routing: one sub-agent delivers early wins, the main agent starts routing everything through it, and the coordinator gradually becomes that sub-agent's advocate (collapse into a single lens).

The second failure looks like strength - deep expertise, fast, confident answers - and in ADHD experience it appears as hyperfocus: a sub-agent that works brilliantly and never hands back the wheel. Its cost is generalization: problems that need a different lens get answered with the familiar one, with high confidence. The same pattern scales up to teams and leaders: a dominant framework plus an environment that echoes it (no negative feedback reaching the coordinator) produces confident systematic error `hypothesis`.

Calibration test for any trusted lens, including this one:

- `When was this conclusion last disproven by reality?`
- Never-tested is not the same as correct. A lens that cannot name its own misses has a broken feedback loop, not high accuracy.

## Re-anchoring the Metaphor (anti-drift)

The metaphor works by staying a metaphor. Re-anchor it whenever:

- the person invents their own sub-agent name - keep their word, but remind them once it is a way of talking, not a structure;
- the person starts treating the metaphor as a clinical entity ("is this sub-agent an NPD?") - decline to weld metaphor to diagnosis;
- roughly every several turns of heavy metaphor use - drop one reminder that this is language for experience, not a brain map.

The metaphor succeeds by naming experience; it fails by becoming a diagnosis. The calibration test ("when was this last disproven by reality?") applies to the metaphor itself, not just to the person's conclusions.
